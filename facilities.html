Experimental capabilities

Overview of current laboratory capabilities, wider UPM/shared infrastructure and specialist methods accessed through collaboration.

Core at LISA · UPM

The in-house experimental backbone

The Laboratory of Sanitary and Environmental Engineering (LISA) provides the microbiological, molecular, analytical and photochemical core used for the group’s mechanistic water research.

Molecular

Targeted environmental molecular microbiology

Two qPCR instruments, automated DNA extraction (~96 samples/hour), 96-well nucleic-acid quantification, PCR/RT-qPCR workflows, electrophoresis and gene imaging.

  • ARG/MGE targets
  • eDNA
  • large campaigns
  • QC

Microbiology

Culture, phenotype & regrowth

Class I/II microbiological facilities, cultivation/incubation capacity, clean benches, microscopy, low-temperature storage, centrifugation and a multimode microplate reader supporting fluorescence, bioluminescence and chemiluminescence assays.

  • ARB
  • biofilms
  • viability
  • stress response

Photochemistry

Controlled light and AOP/AORP experiments

UVC, UVB, UVA and visible-light sources, solar simulators, radiometers, pyranometers and laboratory photoreactors for controlled treatment and environmental-fate experiments.

  • solar
  • photo-Fenton
  • PMS/PS
  • kinetics

Water analytics

Aqueous-matrix characterization

HPLC, Total Organic Carbon and Kjeldahl analysis together with UV–Vis/spectroscopic measurements and routine physicochemical monitoring.

  • HPLC
  • TOC
  • Kjeldahl N
  • UV–Vis

Process monitoring

Routine treatment & water-quality measurements

pH, conductivity, turbidity, dissolved oxygen, BOD/COD and natural/wastewater chemistry kits, supported by thermostatic, ultrasonic and centrifugation equipment.

  • matrix effects
  • QA/QC
  • real waters

Integration

Mechanism → biological endpoint → engineering decision

The real capability is the ability to run chemical, photochemical, microbial and molecular endpoints within the same experimental design.

  • operating windows
  • benchmarking
  • validation

Wider UPM / shared project infrastructure

Additional methods available through the broader UPM research environment

These capacities appear in recent UPM project proposals as shared or cross-group infrastructure. They should not be read as claiming that every instrument is physically housed inside LISA.

Advanced chemical analysis

GC and GC-MS, IR/ATR-FTIR and complementary chromatographic/spectroscopic platforms used across UPM project teams.

Toxicity & biodegradability

Zahn–Wellens, Microtox and related experimental platforms for treatment-safety and biodegradability assessment.

Pilot / process platforms

Ozonation–UV systems, membrane filtration, electrocoagulation and other lab-to-pilot treatment infrastructure used within project collaborations.

Materials characterization

ATR-FTIR, TGA/DSC, micro-hardness, permeability and related methods supporting polymeric and photocatalytic-material work.

Environmental modelling & data

Structured experimental datasets, kinetic interpretation and project-level integration with modelling/ML partners; the group contributes the environmental-process layer rather than claiming core algorithm development.

External specialist platforms when needed

Advanced microscopy, flow cytometry, metagenomics/sequencing and specialist bioinformatics are accessed through institutional cores or collaborators rather than treated as routine in-house capabilities.

What the platform enables

High-throughput, state-aware treatment studies

  • Targeted AMR campaigns: high-throughput extraction + qPCR across pre/post-treatment samples and multiple matrices.
  • Phenotype–genotype coupling: connect culturability, regrowth, biofilm and stress response to targeted molecular endpoints.
  • Mechanistic treatment comparison: compare oxidative/reductive/light-assisted conditions using both microbial and chemical evidence.
  • Real-water validation: move beyond simplified matrices toward operating windows and robustness criteria.
  • Model-ready evidence: generate structured experimental data with defined controls, metadata and process variables.

Claim boundary

Targeted molecular analytics ≠ an omics core

The current platform supports targeted environmental molecular microbiology exceptionally well. Shotgun metagenomics, full mobilome reconstruction, advanced HGT quantification and specialist bioinformatics remain collaboration domains.

Likewise, equipment proposed for pending/rejected applications is not listed here as existing infrastructure.
