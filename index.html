Environmental engineering for safer, more resilient water systems.

How I frame environmental problems

A connected research architecture

I am interested in what happens between a treatment mechanism and its real environmental consequence — not only whether a target disappears.

  1. 01MechanismReactive species, redox pathways, light–matter interactions
  2. 02Biological stateViability, stress response, resistance, biofilms, regrowth
  3. 03Treatment historyHow upstream exposure changes downstream susceptibility
  4. 04Environmental fatePersistence, attenuation, transformation and mobility
  5. 05Exposure interfaceWater, wastewater, crops, biofilms and connected compartments
  6. 06ImplementationOperating windows, validation, decision criteria and KPIs

Research

Scientific capabilities built around problems, not labels

The common thread is mechanism-to-application environmental engineering: understand what controls performance, then test whether it survives contact with realistic water matrices and biological complexity.

01

Environmental AMR & microbial fate

Antibiotic-resistant bacteria and genes, microbial stress, regrowth, biofilms, environmental persistence and the distinction between molecular signal and biologically relevant risk.

  • ARB / ARG
  • qPCR
  • regrowth
  • biofilms
  • One Health

02

Advanced oxidation & photochemistry

Mechanistic analysis of solar and UV-driven treatment, photo-Fenton/Fenton chemistry, persulfate and peroxymonosulfate systems, reactive intermediates and matrix effects.

  • AOPs
  • photo-Fenton
  • ROS
  • sunlight
  • kinetics

03

Water reuse & treatment validation

Comparative assessment of treatment configurations under realistic wastewater and reuse conditions, combining chemical, microbial and operational endpoints.

  • reuse
  • benchmarking
  • real matrices
  • KPIs
  • robustness

04

Contaminant–microorganism interactions

How contaminants, metals and treatment stressors modify microbial behavior, resistance, survival and community-relevant outcomes in water systems.

  • CECs

  • selection
  • microbial response

05

Functional materials & scientific gatekeeping

Microbiological, mechanistic and safety validation of catalytic or functional materials before process progression — linking material performance to treatment relevance rather than testing materials in isolation.

  • photocatalysis
  • disinfection
  • toxicity
  • DBPs
  • go/no-go criteria

Experimental intelligence for digital water

Define meaningful environmental variables, generate structured chemical and microbiological datasets, and provide mechanistic interpretation for predictive and decision-support models.

  • data design

  • decision support

Selected funded research

Projects as evidence of capability

A few examples showing how the research has moved between mechanism, intervention, environmental fate and implementation.

JPIAMRPI, UPM partner

ARPHILAKE

Combating Antibiotic Resistance in Philippine Lakes

One Health intervention research linking environmental AMR monitoring with upstream treatment of hospital and farm effluents, solar/light-assisted processes and environmental fate.

What it demonstrates: environmental AMR intervention, international consortium delivery, ARB/ARG treatment and fate modelling.

AEIPrincipal Investigator

REDDIS

Reductive processes as the Achilles’ Heel of bacteria in wastewater disinfection and natural waters

Mechanistic work on intracellular and environmental redox stress, bacterial defense pathways, advanced oxidation/reduction processes and predictive environmental modelling.

What it demonstrates: mechanism-oriented microbiology, redox chemistry, gene-response thinking and natural attenuation.

National coordinated projectCo-PI, UPM subproject

DIGIT4WATER

Digital tools and water-reclamation technologies for urban-water-cycle resilience

Integration of UVC-based water reclamation, contaminants, pathogens and AMR measurements with data structures designed for machine-learning models and decision-support tools.

What it demonstrates: the interface between wet-lab environmental science, structured datasets and digital-water modelling.

H2020WP4 co-lead / leading-partner contribution

WATERSPOUTT

Water — Sustainable Point-of-Use Treatment Technologies

International research on decentralized drinking-water treatment, with a major contribution to disinfection and treatment enhancement under resource-constrained conditions.

What it demonstrates: international WP leadership, solar disinfection, technology validation and translation to practical treatment systems.

Experimental capacity

About

Environmental engineering across chemistry, microbiology and application

Dr. Stefanos Giannakis is Profesor Titular de Universidad (i3) at the Universidad Politécnica de Madrid (UPM), ETSI de Caminos, Canales y Puertos, Department of Civil Engineering: Hydraulics, Energy and Environment, and Deputy Director of the UPM Centre for R&D&I in Civil, Intelligent and Sustainable Infrastructures (CIVILis).

His academic trajectory combines civil and environmental engineering with research experience in chemical engineering, environmental chemistry and microbiology. Before becoming Profesor Titular, he was a Ramón y Cajal Fellow at UPM and previously worked at EPFL in Switzerland, with additional research experience including the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign and the University of Torino.

He holds a jointly supervised PhD in Environmental Engineering from Aristotle University of Thessaloniki and Universitat Politècnica de Catalunya, together with degrees in Civil/Hydraulic/Environmental Engineering and Environmental Protection & Sustainable Development.
